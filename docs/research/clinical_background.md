# Clinical Background Research

## Why This Patient Profile (HTN + T2DM + CKD)

The demo patient, 62-year-old male with hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and
early CKD, is deliberately chosen because this comorbidity triad:

1. **Is extremely common**, affects 26 million Americans. Judges will recognize it.
2. **Has a clear causal chain**, uncontrolled hypertension → glomerulosclerosis → CKD is a textbook clinical pathway. Easy to explain.
3. **Has guideline gaps**, the ADA, JNC, and KDIGO all have specific (sometimes conflicting) recommendations for this exact triad. Good material for the guidelines tool.
4. **Produces individually-normal-but-together-alarming signals**, BP 138/88 is "pre-hypertensive," creatinine 1.3 is "normal high," fatigue is nonspecific. Together: concerning CKD progression. Perfect for the silent deterioration demo.

## Key Clinical Facts Used in Demo

### Creatinine 1.3 mg/dL
- Normal range: 0.7-1.2 mg/dL (male)
- At 1.3: technically slightly elevated but within lab "normal" for many labs
- Trending: 1.1 (2022) → 1.3 (2026) = 18% increase over 4 years
- Clinical significance: In a patient with known CKD + HTN, any upward trend matters

### BP 138/88 mmHg
- ACC/AHA 2017 guidelines: Stage 1 hypertension starts at 130/80
- JNC 8 (older): Stage 1 starts at 140/90 (this reading is technically "normal")
- In a diabetic + CKD patient: target is <130/80 per ADA 2024
- At 138/88: this patient is above their personalized target even if "normal" by old guidelines

### HbA1c 7.8%
- ADA target for most T2DM patients: <7.0%
- At 7.8%: suboptimal glycemic control → accelerates CKD progression and cardiovascular risk
- Suggests medication adjustment or adherence issue

### The Pattern Together
"Each of these signals is technically normal. But together, rising creatinine, borderline BP in a patient whose target is lower, suboptimal HbA1c, and two notes mentioning fatigue, this is a textbook early CKD stress pattern. A busy clinician reviewing each chart element independently might miss it. An AI reasoning holistically would not."

## Relevant Guidelines

### KDIGO 2022 (CKD)
- Nephrology referral when: eGFR <30, or rapid decline, or uncertainty about diagnosis
- At CKD Stage 2 (eGFR 60-89): routine monitoring, may defer referral
- But: in diabetic patients with rising creatinine, earlier referral is reasonable

### ADA 2024 (Diabetes + CKD)
- SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin) recommended for CKD + T2DM, nephroprotective
- BP target: <130/80 mmHg
- Annual urine albumin-creatinine ratio monitoring
- This patient is not on SGLT2i → guideline gap

### JNC 8 (Hypertension)
- First-line for CKD: ACE inhibitor or ARB (lisinopril qualifies)
- But: is lisinopril dose optimized given BP 138/88?
